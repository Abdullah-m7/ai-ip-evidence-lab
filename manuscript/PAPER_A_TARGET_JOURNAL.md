# Paper A — Target Journal Decision

## Primary target

**Artificial Intelligence and Law (Springer Nature)**

## Why this is the best first target

Paper A is not primarily a descriptive copyright article. Its central contribution is a computational/legal methodology: translating legal conditions into an evidence contract, separating machine-observable facts from open-textured legal judgment, testing interoperability against provenance standards, and adversarially validating the integrity boundary.

The journal’s longstanding scope includes computational models of law and legal reasoning, applications of AI in the legal domain, and the societal/legal impact of AI systems. Recent issues continue to publish work on automatically processable regulation, open-texture in regulations, and empirical/technical legal-AI methods.

## Working manuscript type

Original research article with a legal-computational methods contribution.

## Working title

**From Copyright Exception to Verifiable Evidence: An Evidence Architecture for AI Development under Saudi Arabia’s 2026 Copyright Law**

Use “evidence” rather than “verifiable compliance”. The completed experiments establish evidentiary observability, provenance integrity, semantic boundaries, and falsifiable validation infrastructure. They do not establish an authoritative legal-compliance determination.

## Target contribution statement

> This paper introduces and tests a jurisdiction-specific legal-evidence layer for AI-development uses of copyrighted works. The layer preserves facts needed for later legal review while explicitly preventing cryptographic provenance, signer trust, and machine-readable usage preferences from being treated as legal conclusions.

## Fit risks

The paper may be rejected as too jurisdiction-specific if the Saudi testbed is not connected to a general computational-law problem. The introduction and discussion must therefore make clear that:

1. Saudi law supplies the concrete and unusually explicit evidence problem;
2. the architecture is not a universal copyright ontology;
3. the transferable contribution is the **method** for mapping law → observable evidence → integrity layer → human-judgment boundary.

## Fallback target

**Computer Law & Security Review (CLSR)** if the final manuscript develops into a primarily legal/policy analysis rather than a computational-law methodology.

CLSR explicitly welcomes high-quality legal analysis and new lines of thought in intellectual property and technology law. Its published guidance indicates normal article lengths around 6,000–15,000 words.

## Current decision

**TARGET: Artificial Intelligence and Law**

Do not retarget until the first full manuscript and claim-evidence audit are complete.
