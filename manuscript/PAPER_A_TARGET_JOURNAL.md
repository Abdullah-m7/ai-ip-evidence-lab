# Paper A — Target Journal Decision

## Primary target

**Artificial Intelligence and Law (Springer Nature)**

## Controller decision

**TARGET CONFIRMED AFTER FULL MANUSCRIPT AND CLAIM/CITATION AUDIT.**

Paper A is not primarily a descriptive copyright article. Its central contribution is a computational/legal methodology: translating fact-conditioned legal provisions into an evidence contract, separating machine-observable facts from open-textured legal judgment, testing interoperability against provenance standards, and adversarially validating the integrity boundary.

The journal’s established purpose includes computational models of law and legal reasoning, applications of AI in the legal domain, and the effects of legal AI systems on the profession and society. Recent articles on automatically processable regulation, open texture, legislation encoding, legal compliance checking, and algorithmic disclosure show direct methodological adjacency.

## Manuscript type

Original research article with a legal-computational methods and technical-validation contribution.

## Working title

**From Copyright Exception to Verifiable Evidence: An Evidence Architecture for AI Development under Saudi Arabia’s 2026 Copyright Law**

The title uses “evidence” rather than “verifiable compliance”. The completed experiments establish evidentiary observability, provenance integrity, semantic boundaries, and falsifiable validation infrastructure. They do not establish an authoritative legal-compliance determination.

## Target contribution statement

> This paper introduces and tests a jurisdiction-specific legal-evidence layer for AI-development uses of copyrighted works. The layer preserves facts needed for later legal review while explicitly preventing cryptographic provenance, signer trust, and machine-readable usage preferences from being treated as legal conclusions.

## Fit strengths

1. The work presents executable legal/technical methodology, not only doctrinal commentary.
2. The legal-to-evidence decomposition directly engages the journal’s computational-law tradition.
3. The open-texture boundary is implemented rather than mentioned abstractly.
4. The experiments are reproducible and adversarial, while the human-study claim is explicitly deferred.
5. Saudi Arabia supplies a new and concrete legal testbed, while the transferable claim is the mapping and boundary method.

## Fit risks and mitigations

### Risk: too jurisdiction-specific

**Mitigation:** the manuscript distinguishes the Saudi field set from the general method and includes comparative sources from the EU, United States, United Kingdom, Japan, and broader scholarship.

### Risk: too engineering-heavy for a legal journal

**Mitigation:** every experiment answers a legal-evidentiary question, and the paper explains why content provenance, rights signaling, evidence readiness, and legal judgment are different layers.

### Risk: insufficient external validity

**Mitigation:** synthetic experiments are labeled as such; the paper includes a prespecified, separately governed human-validation path and states that no human effect has been established.

### Risk: manuscript mistaken for automated compliance

**Mitigation:** the title, abstract, gate names, claim matrix, source audit, and CI audit all prohibit that characterization.

## Fallback target

**Computer Law & Security Review (CLSR)** remains the fallback if external review concludes that the final paper is primarily a copyright-policy/evidentiary analysis rather than a computational-law contribution.

Retargeting should follow an explicit fit review, not an attempt to evade substantive reviewer criticism.
