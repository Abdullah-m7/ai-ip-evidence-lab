## Reviewer-facing takeaway

A reviewer can legitimately say that **reviewability, evidentiary records, training-data provenance, and C2PA-based rights management all predate IPEL**. Paper A should concede that point explicitly.

The defensible response is not priority rhetoric. It is to show that the paper performs a combination the close works do not demonstrate together:

1. starts from an enacted copyright rule with an express work/source/purpose/date record-retention duty;
2. decomposes the whole AI-use pathway into structural, evidence-supported, and judgment-sensitive conditions;
3. implements a machine-readable use-event contract and fail-closed evidence gate;
4. maps that contract against C2PA/CAWG semantics rather than merely recommending provenance;
5. empirically demonstrates false-equivalence cases where `allowed/trusted/valid` cannot cure separate adverse legal facts;
6. tests real hard bindings and corruption classes;
7. records the limits of cross-validator diversity and human validation instead of converting them into claims.

If the revised manuscript makes those seven points unmistakable, the high-overlap works narrow the contribution but do not duplicate it.
